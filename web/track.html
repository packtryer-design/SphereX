Pluto, the original Planet X

In 1930 Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto by blinking two photographs: stars stay put, Pluto moves. Here are NASA SPHEREx's own images of Pluto over three days. The ring is where NASA JPL's orbit predicts it, worked out independently of these images.

Could SPHEREx find a new planet?

A planet far beyond Pluto would be too faint to see in any single image. Astronomers add images up while shifting each one along a guessed path: only something moving that way builds up, while the noise averages out. We tried every plausible path, blind.

Searching…

To test how far this goes, we planted a simulated planet, moving like a world 300 times farther from the Sun than Earth, into the real images, and searched again.

Black patches: bright stars masked out before searching. The planted planet is simulated, to test the method; this is not a discovery.
