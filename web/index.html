    Observations

    Is a difference real?

    Before reading a change between two visits as something happening in space, rule out these first:

    • Different detectors. SPHEREx has six detectors, each covering a different range of infrared colours. If two visits don't use the same detectors, they show the object in different colours. Blink warns you when that happens, and its Difference view only subtracts detectors both visits share.
    • Noise. Faint, grainy differences that don't form a shape are usually noise. A real change keeps its shape from one visit to the next.
    • Partial visits. Some visits caught only part of the field, or used fewer exposures. They are noisier and can have blank edges.
    • Processing. All visits share one alignment grid and one brightness scale, so one visit can't look brighter just because it was scaled differently. Bright stars can still leave artifacts, such as ghosts and spikes, that differ between visits.

    Things that really do change over months: nearby stars moving across the sky, variable stars, and asteroids or comets passing through. A nebula's gas is light-years across, so it won't visibly change between visits.
